The first three are each won by a different route, which is why all three are implemented rather than one. The first question of a session is slower: a route that matches by meaning has to embed the question, and the embedding model loads on first use.

Why a template writes this

The answer is assembled from the retrieved evidence by a template, not by a language model. Measured on this machine, same question and same evidence: the template answered in 37 ms with all three sections, the 3B model took 107 seconds and silently dropped the interpretation and the response. The gateway would time out before it finished.

A template cannot hallucinate, because it has no way to produce a sentence that was not assembled from retrieved facts. The model stays in the system as a measured alternative, run against the graph service directly rather than offered as a button here.

Every answer comes back in three parts: what was observed, what it might mean, and what to do about it. They are kept apart on purpose. The events are facts, the threat is a hypothesis, and somebody suspending a real merchant's payouts needs to know which is which.

Underneath each answer is everything that was retrieved to produce it: the nodes with how closely they matched, the relationships walked, the passages quoted with their document and revision, and the query that ran.
